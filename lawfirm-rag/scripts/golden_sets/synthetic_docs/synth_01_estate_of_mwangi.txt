JUDGMENT

Delivered on 9 May 2026 before Hon. Lady Justice H. N. Oduya

INTRODUCTION

1. This judgment determines a summons for confirmation of grant dated 3 October 2024 and a protest to that confirmation filed by the Objector on 21 November 2024. The deceased, Wanjiru Kamau Mwangi, died intestate on 14 March 2019 at Nakuru Level Five Hospital. A certificate of death, serial number 0448812, was produced in evidence and its authenticity was not challenged by any party.

2. Letters of administration intestate were issued to the Petitioner, Grace Nyambura Mwangi, who is the widow of the deceased, on 12 July 2023. The Objector, Peter Kariuki Mwangi, is the eldest son of the deceased. The Interested Party, Lucy Wairimu Njoroge, claims to have purchased a portion of the deceased's land during his lifetime and seeks to have that portion excised before distribution.

3. The court heard oral evidence from five witnesses over three days, namely 4 February, 5 February and 19 March 2026. The parties thereafter filed written submissions. Counsel for the Petitioner, Mr. A. Gichuru, filed submissions dated 2 April 2026. Counsel for the Objector, Ms. R. Atieno, filed submissions dated 10 April 2026. The Interested Party acted in person.

THE ESTATE

4. It is not disputed that the deceased left the following assets:
(a) Land parcel Nakuru/Bahati/1123 measuring approximately 5.0 acres, on which the matrimonial home stands;
(b) Land parcel Nakuru/Solai/774 measuring approximately 4.0 acres, used for maize and dairy farming;
(c) 2,400 shares in Bahati Highlands Dairy Cooperative Society;
(d) A motor vehicle registration number KCB 412T, a Toyota Probox;
(e) A bank account at the Nakuru branch of a commercial bank with a balance of KES 1,186,400 as at the date of death.

5. The deceased also left a liability, being an outstanding loan of KES 350,000 owed to the Bahati Highlands Dairy Cooperative Society, which the Petitioner testified she has continued to service from the proceeds of milk sales.

THE BENEFICIARIES

6. The deceased was survived by the Petitioner as his only wife and by four children of the marriage, namely Peter Kariuki Mwangi (the Objector), born 1984; Esther Wangui Mwangi, born 1987; Samuel Ndungu Mwangi, born 1991; and Mary Wanjiku Mwangi, born 1996. All the children are adults. No party alleged the existence of any other dependant within the meaning of section 29 of the Law of Succession Act.

THE PROPOSED DISTRIBUTION AND THE PROTEST

7. The Petitioner proposed that parcel Nakuru/Bahati/1123 be transmitted to her absolutely, that parcel Nakuru/Solai/774 be shared equally among the four children, that the cooperative shares and the motor vehicle vest in her, and that the bank balance be applied first to the cooperative loan with the remainder shared equally between her and the four children.

8. The Objector protested on three grounds. First, he contended that the widow should hold the Bahati parcel only as a life interest, with the remainder passing to the children in equal shares, rather than receiving it absolutely. Second, he contended that he had developed a portion of the Solai parcel with a zero-grazing unit and borehole at his own cost of approximately KES 900,000 and should therefore receive the developed one acre in addition to his equal share. Third, he alleged that the Petitioner had withdrawn KES 600,000 from the bank account before the grant was issued, amounting to intermeddling under section 45 of the Act.

9. The Interested Party claimed that she purchased 0.5 acres of the Solai parcel from the deceased in 2017 for KES 450,000 and produced a handwritten agreement dated 6 June 2017 and two bank deposit slips.

SUMMARY OF THE EVIDENCE

The Petitioner's case

9A. The Petitioner (PW1) adopted her affidavit in support of the summons for confirmation sworn on 3 October 2024 as her evidence in chief. She testified that she married the deceased under Kikuyu customary law in 1982 and that the marriage was later solemnised at a church in Bahati in 1990. A marriage certificate, serial number 77120, was produced. She stated that she and the deceased built the matrimonial home on the Bahati parcel in 1988 from the proceeds of their dairy business, and that she has lived there continuously since.

9B. On the Solai parcel, PW1 testified that the deceased used it for maize and dairy farming, keeping eleven dairy cows at the time of his death. She stated that after the death she continued the dairy business with the help of her youngest son, Samuel Ndungu Mwangi, and that the herd had since reduced to seven cows because she sold four to meet household expenses and the cooperative loan repayments. She produced milk delivery records from the cooperative showing average monthly deliveries of 1,450 litres in 2020, falling to 980 litres in 2025.

9C. On the bank withdrawals, PW1 testified that the deceased fell ill in February 2019 and was admitted to hospital for nineteen days before he died. She stated that the hospital would not release the body until the bill was settled, and that she withdrew money using the deceased's ATM card, the PIN to which he had shared with her during his illness. She conceded that she had no authority to withdraw the money but said she did not know she needed a court order. On cross-examination by Ms. Atieno, she conceded that she paid the school fees for Mary from the same account in May 2019 but said that the deceased had promised Mary that he would pay her final year's fees.

9D. PW2, Samuel Ndungu Mwangi, supported the Petitioner's evidence. He testified that he works on the Solai parcel daily and that the Objector rarely visits. On cross-examination he admitted that he did not object when the Objector built the zero-grazing unit in 2021 and that he had used the borehole water for his own crops.

The Objector's case

9E. The Objector (OW1) testified that he is an agricultural extension officer employed by the County Government of Nakuru. He stated that in 2021 he constructed a zero-grazing unit for six cows and sank a borehole 180 metres deep on the eastern one acre of the Solai parcel, using a loan of KES 700,000 from his Sacco and KES 200,000 of his savings. He produced the borehole contractor's invoice dated 14 June 2021 and a Water Resources Authority permit dated 2 August 2021.

9F. OW1 testified that he informed his mother of the developments by telephone and that she did not object, but conceded that no written consent was obtained from his siblings. He stated that he objects to the widow receiving the Bahati parcel absolutely because he fears that she will transfer it to Samuel, who lives with her, to the exclusion of the other children.

9G. OW2, Esther Wangui Mwangi, the second child of the deceased, testified in support of the Objector on the issue of the life interest only. She stated that she has no objection to her brother keeping the developed acre provided that it is counted as part of his share. She told the court that she lives in Eldoret and works as a nurse.

The Interested Party's case

9H. The Interested Party testified that she runs a hardware shop at Solai trading centre and that the deceased approached her in 2017 because he needed money to pay hospital bills for an earlier illness. She stated that they agreed on a price of KES 450,000 for half an acre on the western boundary of the Solai parcel adjoining the road, and that she paid in two instalments of KES 250,000 on 6 June 2017 and KES 200,000 on 30 August 2017. She conceded that no application was made to the Land Control Board and that she never took possession of the land, as the deceased asked her to wait until after harvest. She called no witness to the agreement.

9I. The area chief, who was summoned by the court, testified that the deceased had mentioned to him in 2018 that he "owed Mama Lucy for a piece of land" but that he had never seen any agreement. The court notes that the chief's letter dated 11 November 2022 lists the Interested Party as a "purchaser," but the chief conceded that he relied only on what the Interested Party told him.

ISSUES FOR DETERMINATION

10. From the pleadings, evidence and submissions, the court identified the following issues:
(i) Whether the Interested Party has proved a valid purchaser's interest in a portion of parcel Nakuru/Solai/774;
(ii) Whether the widow should take parcel Nakuru/Bahati/1123 absolutely or as a life interest;
(iii) Whether the Objector's developments on the Solai parcel entitle him to a larger share;
(iv) Whether the Petitioner intermeddled with the estate; and
(v) The appropriate mode of distribution and the order on costs.

ANALYSIS

Issue (i): The Interested Party's claim

11. A person claiming to have purchased land from a deceased person bears the burden of proving the sale on a balance of probabilities. The handwritten agreement produced by the Interested Party was not witnessed, did not describe the portion sold by reference to any survey or sketch, and was not accompanied by consent of the Land Control Board, which is mandatory for agricultural land under the Land Control Act. Without such consent the transaction is void for all purposes after six months from the date of the agreement.

12. The court therefore finds that the Interested Party has not proved an enforceable interest in the Solai parcel. However, the evidence of the two deposit slips totalling KES 450,000 was credible, and the court finds that the sum was paid to the deceased. The Interested Party is accordingly a creditor of the estate for KES 450,000, which shall be refunded from the estate before distribution, without interest.

Issue (ii): Life interest or absolute transmission

13. Section 35 of the Law of Succession Act provides that where an intestate leaves one surviving spouse and a child or children, the surviving spouse is entitled to the personal and household effects absolutely and to a life interest in the whole residue of the net intestate estate. The life interest terminates upon remarriage where the surviving spouse is a widow.

14. The Petitioner urged the court to depart from section 35 on the ground that the children are adults who are economically independent. While the court has a discretion in distribution, that discretion must be exercised within the statutory framework. The court is not persuaded that the circumstances justify giving the widow absolute ownership of the matrimonial land to the exclusion of the children. Accordingly, the Petitioner shall hold parcel Nakuru/Bahati/1123 as a life interest, and upon the determination of that interest the parcel shall devolve to the four children in equal shares.

14A. The court has also considered the Objector's fear that the Petitioner may transfer the Bahati parcel to Samuel Ndungu Mwangi to the exclusion of the other children. A life interest does not confer a power of sale or transfer of the capital. Under the proviso to section 37 of the Act, a surviving spouse holding a life interest may not sell any immovable property comprised in the estate without the consent of all co-holders of the interest or the leave of the court. The Objector's fear is therefore addressed by the nature of the interest itself, and the court directs the Land Registrar to register a restriction against parcel Nakuru/Bahati/1123 to that effect.

14B. The Petitioner may, however, continue to occupy, farm, and take the income of the Bahati parcel for her maintenance during her life, including the rent from the two-room extension that she lets to a teacher at the local primary school at KES 6,500 per month. The children shall not interfere with her quiet enjoyment of the parcel during her lifetime.

14C. The court has noted the submission by Ms. Atieno that the Petitioner should be required to account for the income from the Bahati parcel to the children. The holder of a life interest is entitled to the income as of right and is not accountable for it. The account directed at paragraph 19E below relates only to the period before confirmation and to the assets that form part of the residue.

Issue (iii): The Objector's developments

15. The Objector proved, through receipts and the evidence of a borehole contractor, that he spent approximately KES 900,000 on a zero-grazing unit and borehole on the Solai parcel. However, the developments were made without the consent of the other beneficiaries and after the death of the deceased. A beneficiary who develops estate property without the consent of co-beneficiaries does so at his own risk.

16. In fairness, and to avoid unjust enrichment of the other beneficiaries, the court directs that the one acre on which the developments stand shall form part of the Objector's share, but that the Objector's share shall not exceed his equal portion. The developed acre will therefore be counted towards, and not added to, his entitlement.

Issue (iv): Intermeddling

17. The bank statement produced by the Petitioner shows that KES 600,000 was withdrawn between April and June 2019, before the grant was issued. The Petitioner explained that the money was used to pay for the funeral (KES 310,000), hospital bills (KES 172,000) and school fees for Mary Wanjiku Mwangi, who was then a university student (KES 118,000). Receipts were produced for the funeral and hospital expenses.

18. Under section 45 of the Act, dealing with the free property of a deceased person without authority is an offence, but reasonable funeral expenses are a proper charge on the estate. The court finds that the funeral and hospital expenses, totalling KES 482,000, were reasonable and are allowed as a charge on the estate. The KES 118,000 applied to school fees shall be treated as an advancement to Mary Wanjiku Mwangi and brought into account against her share of the bank balance. The court declines to find that the Petitioner intermeddled with a dishonest intent.

Issue (v): Distribution

19. After deducting the refund to the Interested Party (KES 450,000) and the outstanding cooperative loan (KES 350,000), and noting that the funeral and hospital expenses were already met from the withdrawn funds, the net cash in the estate is the balance remaining in the bank account. The court directs that the residue of the bank balance be shared equally among the five beneficiaries, subject to the advancement to Mary Wanjiku Mwangi.

Directions on subdivision and implementation

19A. To give effect to the distribution of the Solai parcel, the court appoints Mr. Joseph Kirui, a licensed surveyor practising in Nakuru, to survey and subdivide parcel Nakuru/Solai/774 into four portions of approximately 1.0 acre each. The survey shall place the portion of Peter Kariuki Mwangi on the eastern side so that it includes the zero-grazing unit and borehole. The remaining three portions shall be balloted for among Esther Wangui Mwangi, Samuel Ndungu Mwangi and Mary Wanjiku Mwangi in the presence of the Deputy Registrar.

19B. The surveyor's fees, the costs of the Land Control Board consent for subdivision, and the registration fees shall be met from the estate before the residue of the bank balance is shared. If the bank balance is insufficient, the costs shall be borne by the four children in equal shares.

19C. The borehole shall serve all four portions. Each child shall have a right to draw water for domestic use and for not more than five dairy cows, and the four children shall share the cost of maintaining the pump and the Water Resources Authority permit equally. Peter Kariuki Mwangi shall grant the necessary wayleave for a water pipe along the boundary of his portion.

19D. The motor vehicle KCB 412T, which was valued at KES 650,000 by a motor assessor in January 2026, shall not be sold during the life of the Petitioner without the consent of the majority of the children.

19E. The Petitioner, as administrator, shall file a full and accurate account of the administration of the estate within six (6) months of this judgment, including an account of the milk proceeds received since 2019 and of the four cows sold.

DISPOSITION

20. For the foregoing reasons, the court makes the following orders:
(a) The protest by the Objector dated 21 November 2024 is allowed in part.
(b) The claim of the Interested Party to 0.5 acres of parcel Nakuru/Solai/774 is dismissed; the estate shall instead refund her KES 450,000 within 90 days of this judgment.
(c) The grant of letters of administration issued on 12 July 2023 is hereby confirmed.
(d) Parcel Nakuru/Bahati/1123 shall be held by Grace Nyambura Mwangi for her life, and thereafter shall devolve to the four children in equal shares.
(e) Parcel Nakuru/Solai/774 shall be subdivided into four equal portions for the four children, with the one acre containing the zero-grazing unit and borehole forming part of the portion of Peter Kariuki Mwangi.
(f) The 2,400 cooperative shares and motor vehicle KCB 412T shall vest in Grace Nyambura Mwangi absolutely.
(g) The residue of the bank balance shall be shared equally among the five beneficiaries, with KES 118,000 brought into account against the share of Mary Wanjiku Mwangi.
(h) Each party shall bear its own costs, this being a family dispute.

21. Right of appeal within 30 days.
